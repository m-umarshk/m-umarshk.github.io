# My Last Day of First Semester

<img src="/assets/images/last-day-sem1.jpeg"
alt="Last Day of First Semester"
style="width:100%; border-radius:12px; margin-bottom:20px;">

There is something special about the last day of a semester. After months of lectures, assignments, lab sessions, quizzes, and everything in between, you finally reach the finish line. For me and my entire class, the last day of our first semester was not just a day of relief — it was a day of genuine celebration, gratitude, and reflection. It was the kind of day that reminds you why you chose this journey in the first place.

The highlight of the day was something simple but deeply meaningful — we cut a cake together as a class to celebrate the completion of our first semester. It might sound like a small gesture, but in that moment it felt like so much more. Every person in that room had gone through the same struggles, the same late nights, the same confusion when a concept would not click, and the same satisfaction when things finally started to make sense. Cutting that cake together was our way of acknowledging that we had made it through, as a class and as a team.

After the celebration, we gathered for group photos. The entire class came together, laughing and talking, and for a little while it felt like all the stress of the semester had completely lifted. Looking around at my classmates in those moments, I realized how much we had all grown together over the course of just a few months. We had started the semester as strangers and were ending it as people who had shared a real experience together. Those photos will always be a reminder of where this journey began.

Throughout the semester, the guidance of Dr. Bilal Ahmad had shaped not just what we learned but how we approached our work. His teaching went beyond Python syntax and machine learning algorithms. He consistently pushed us to take our work seriously, to think about the real-world impact of what we were building, and to never treat any task as unimportant. That lesson stayed with me all semester and became even clearer on that last day when I looked back at everything we had accomplished. For anyone interested in following Dr. Bilal Ahmad's academic work and research, his [Google Scholar profile](https://scholar.google.com.au/citations?user=8nZ0jVkAAAAJ&hl=en) and [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/drbilalphd/) are worth exploring.

One piece of advice that I carried with me from the entire semester experience was something that became clearer through every challenge I faced — never take anything carelessly. Whether it was a small assignment, a lab task, or a concept that seemed too simple to bother understanding deeply, everything mattered. The moments where I had rushed through something or not given it my full attention always came back to remind me that carelessness has a cost. Going into the next semester, that lesson was at the front of my mind.

Thinking about the second semester on that last day brought a mix of excitement and determination. The first semester had shown me what university-level Computer Engineering actually demands. It had shown me the gap between where I started and where I needed to be, and more importantly, it had shown me that the gap could be closed with consistent effort and the right attitude. I made a promise to myself that in the next semester I would work harder, prepare earlier, and approach every subject with the seriousness it deserved.

The first semester had given me so much — a foundation in Python programming, an introduction to machine learning, the experience of presenting at an exhibition in front of senior academics, and perhaps most importantly, the confidence that I belonged in this field. None of it came easily, but all of it was worth it. As I walked away from that last day, cake in my memory and camera roll full of group photos, I felt genuinely proud of what we had all achieved together.

First semester — completed. Second semester — bring it on.
