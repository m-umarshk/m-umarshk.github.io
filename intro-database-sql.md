# Introduction to Database and SQL

<img src="/assets/images/intro-database-sql.jpeg"
alt="Introduction to Database and SQL"
style="width:100%; border-radius:12px; margin-bottom:20px;">

One of the most interesting subjects I studied during my second semester at UET Faisalabad was Database Systems. At first, I thought databases would be very difficult because there were many new concepts and technical terms. However, as the semester progressed, I realized that databases are one of the most important parts of modern software systems. Almost every application we use today depends on databases — social media platforms, online shopping websites, banking systems, hospitals, universities, and many other systems use databases to store and manage information. Learning how databases work gave me a better understanding of how real-world software systems operate behind the scenes.

The first thing we learned was the basic concept of a database — an organized collection of data that can be stored, managed, and accessed efficiently. We learned how information is stored in tables consisting of rows and columns, and how different tables are connected through relationships. Understanding these relationships helped us see how large amounts of data can be managed without unnecessary duplication. Some of the core concepts we studied included tables, rows and columns, primary keys, foreign keys, relationships, and data integrity. These concepts became the foundation of our entire database knowledge throughout the semester.

One of the most exciting parts of the course was learning SQL (Structured Query Language) and working with MySQL. Through SQL, we learned how to interact with databases and perform different operations such as creating databases, creating tables, inserting records, updating data, deleting records, and retrieving information using queries. Writing SQL queries was a completely new experience for me. At first, I made many mistakes, but with practice I became more comfortable writing queries and understanding how databases process information. Learning MySQL helped me understand what happens behind the scenes whenever users create accounts, search for information, place orders, or store data in applications.

Another important topic we studied was Database Normalization. At first, normalization seemed confusing because it involved several rules and forms. However, after practicing different examples, I understood its importance in database design. We learned First Normal Form (1NF), Second Normal Form (2NF), and Third Normal Form (3NF). These techniques help reduce data redundancy and improve database efficiency. Through various exercises, we learned how to convert unorganized data into properly structured database tables. Database design became one of the most interesting parts of the subject because it required logical thinking and problem-solving skills — two things that Dr. Bilal Ahmad had consistently emphasized throughout our time in his courses.

Along with theory classes, we also attended database lab sessions which provided practical experience and allowed us to apply the concepts learned in class. The database labs were conducted under the supervision of Dr. Bilal Ahmad. During these sessions, we worked on SQL queries, database creation, table relationships, normalization techniques, and database design. Each lab introduced new concepts and practical exercises where we practiced writing queries, creating databases, inserting records, updating information, and testing database operations. We were also required to prepare and submit lab manuals containing lab objectives, SQL queries, screenshots of outputs, database designs, and observations. Preparing these manuals helped reinforce the concepts learned during each session.

One of the most valuable skills I developed during this course was database design from scratch. We learned how to analyze requirements and create database structures for different scenarios — designing tables, defining relationships, and organizing data efficiently. This process improved my logical thinking and gave me a real sense of how professional database systems are planned before implementation. As the semester progressed, we also worked on database-related projects that gave us the opportunity to apply our theoretical and practical knowledge to real-world problems. Working on these projects also improved my teamwork, planning, and technical skills.

By the end of the semester, Database Systems had become one of the most useful and practical subjects of my second semester. The guidance of Dr. Bilal Ahmad helped us connect theoretical concepts with practical applications and made the learning process far more effective. His approach of always tying academic work to real-world relevance — something he carries through all his teaching and research — made even the more complex topics feel grounded and meaningful. You can explore his research and academic contributions on his [Google Scholar profile](https://scholar.google.com.au/citations?user=8nZ0jVkAAAAJ&hl=en) and connect with him on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/drbilalphd/).

The knowledge I gained from this course will remain an important part of my technical foundation as I continue my Computer Engineering journey. Understanding how information is organized and managed in real-world applications has given me skills that will support me in every software development and engineering project ahead.
