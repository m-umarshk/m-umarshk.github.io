# End of My Second Semester – Memories, BBQ, and What Comes Next

<img src="/assets/images/end-semester2.jpeg"
alt="End of Second Semester"
style="width:100%; border-radius:12px; margin-bottom:20px;">

There is a particular kind of happiness that comes at the end of a semester — not just the relief of finishing exams and assignments, but a deeper sense of accomplishment that builds up over months of hard work and then hits you all at once when it is finally over. When my second semester came to an end, that feeling was stronger than anything I had experienced after my first. Two semesters of Computer Engineering completed. That meant something real to me, and I wanted to celebrate it properly.

The last day of the semester was one I will not forget easily. While the first semester had ended with a cake and group photos during the day, the second semester ended on an even better note — that night, a group of us went out together for a BBQ. Sitting around the table with friends who had been through the same journey, the same projects, the same stressful weeks and late nights, felt like the perfect way to close the chapter. We laughed, we talked about everything we had been through, and for a few hours none of us were thinking about assignments or deadlines. It was just us, good food, and the satisfaction of having made it through another semester together.

We also took photos that night, the way we always do at the end of a semester. Every time I look back at those pictures, I am reminded of how much this group of people has meant to my university experience. The friendships built inside classrooms and labs, through shared frustrations and shared victories, are something no academic result can fully capture. Those moments are as much a part of my education as anything I learned from a textbook.

The second semester had been genuinely demanding. Between Digital Logic Design with the parking counter project that took three weeks of troubleshooting, building the Flappy Bird game in C#, completing the Chronic Kidney Disease Prediction system with a 98% accuracy rate, and keeping up with Database Systems, it had been a busy and challenging few months. But looking back at everything we had accomplished, the difficulty felt worth it. Each project had taught me something different, and together they had pushed my skills further than I had expected going into the semester.

One experience from the semester that did not go the way I had hoped was FESTA — the university sports festival. I had participated in the cricket competition, excited to represent and compete. Unfortunately, we lost in our very first match and were eliminated straight away. It was disappointing, and for a moment the loss stung. But sport has a way of teaching you lessons that the classroom cannot — that you will not always win, that some days are simply not yours, and that the right response to a loss is not regret but preparation. I walked away from that match with a quiet determination to come back stronger.

With two semesters behind me and three months of break ahead, I also found myself doing a lot of thinking about what comes next. The break felt well earned, but I was already looking forward to the third semester with a clear intention to work harder, stay more consistent, and never let things pile up the way they sometimes had before. Every semester had taught me something new about how to be a better student, and I was carrying all of those lessons forward.

The second semester had given me technical skills, real project experience, and memories that will stay with me for a long time. It had shown me what I was capable of when I pushed myself, and it had reminded me of the value of the people around me. As the BBQ night came to an end and we said our goodbyes, I felt grateful — for the semester, for the friends, and for the journey that was still very much ahead of me.

Third semester — I am ready.
